What this is. Three views of one PREDICTED model (PM-001, Team 207 Critical Mass). Robot (the default): put a 0.457 m robot anywhere on the field at any heading, give it a turret, set the release height, backspin and grip, and the page solves the clean-entry window for that exact spot live in your browser (a background worker runs the same flight model), then gives the minimum wheel rpm, the minimum hood angle and the most forgiving shot, each beside its no-spin twin. Single plane: the original model grid (src/shooter_spec.py: 111 speeds × 261 angles × 24 distances × 5 heights per case) for a shot straight down the HIVE tipping plane. Field map: the precomputed windows for every 0.05 m grid position (data/field). Air resistance is included in all three: quadratic drag (Cd), Magnus spin lift capped at a maximum, and spin decay during the flight. Those coefficients are ASSUMED literature values, not POLLEN measurements. The HIVE section is from the official field CAD; tilt 30° is confirmed.

What it is not. No POLLEN drag, spin lift, bounce or launcher grip has been measured. The air cases are literature ranges (pickleball and smooth-sphere data), not POLLEN facts. A shot that touches the HIVE stops here; a bounce can still score (see docs/05). The second HIVE and the A-frame legs are drawn for context only. One clean simulated shot says nothing about repeatability: the tolerance shown is how far speed and angle can drift and still go in. In the Robot view the release point is the robot centre at your height and the turret is assumed to aim exactly (ASSUMED); the cell side walls are not modelled, so windows from a bearing of 35.57° or more are optimistic.
