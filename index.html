How much of the wheel speed the ball gets. Not measured yet; 0.42 is the middle guess. It changes the rpm, not the shot.

How much the air slows the ball and how much backspin lifts it. Literature values, not POLLEN measurements.

Warm, high or damp air is thinner, so the ball slows a little less. Default: 20 °C, sea level, dry air = 1.204 kg/m³ (derived). A venue 1.6 km up moves the hood angle by about half a degree (PREDICTED). The heat map on the floor still uses 1.20.

Real balls lose some drag as they go faster. Nobody has measured this for POLLEN. The smooth-ball curve is for a ball with no holes; the what-if drops drag from the air model's value to 0.33 between Reynolds numbers 30,000 and 50,000 (about 6.4 to 10.7 m/s), so it only matters for fast shots.

A spinning ball can feel more drag (tennis balls do). This adds drag that grows with spin, up to the chosen amount at S = 0.6. The no-spin twin never gets it.

Air moving in the hall, for example from vents. + means toward the HIVE / to the left of the shot / upward. A 0.3 m/s tail draft moves the hood angle by about half a degree (PREDICTED). A sideways draft switches the solve to full 3D flight (slower).

The ball leaves with the shooter's speed plus the robot's speed, so a moving robot needs a different setting. Speeds are the robot's ground speed at the moment of the shot. d0 is how far the ball's exit angle differs from the hood angle (not measured; 0 = the same). With lead off, the turret points at the cell and the ball drifts sideways. The answer also says how much the setting moves per 0.1 m/s. Uses full 3D flight (slower).

Lift table: your own spin-lift curve (pairs of spin ratio S and lift coefficient Cl), straight lines between points, flat after the last. Blank = the air model's capped line. Reverse lift: at slow speed and low spin a smooth ball can lift the wrong way (smooth-sphere data, Kim 2014 / Kray 2012, not POLLEN); below Reynolds 46,000 (about 9.8 m/s) and S 0.5 the lift blends toward minus this value. 0 = off. The no-spin twin never gets lift.

A holed ball drags some air along, so it acts a little heavier: phi 0 to 1 (0 = only the usual added mass of a sphere, 1 = also all the air inside). Blank = off. At phi 1 every force acts about 1.4 % weaker (gravity too). Not measured.

Where these numbers come from

About this model. PREDICTED by a computer model; no POLLEN drag, spin lift, bounce or grip has been measured. Test on a real robot before a match.

What this is. Three views of one PREDICTED model (PM-001, Team 207 Critical Mass). Robot (the default): put a 0.457 m robot anywhere on the field at any heading, give it a turret, set the release height, backspin and grip, and the page solves the clean-entry window for that exact spot live in your browser (a background worker runs the same flight model), then gives the minimum wheel rpm, the minimum hood angle and the most forgiving shot, each beside its no-spin twin. Side view (single plane): the original model grid (src/shooter_spec.py: 111 speeds × 261 angles × 24 distances × 5 heights per case) for a shot straight down the HIVE tipping plane. Field map: the precomputed windows for every 0.05 m grid position (data/field). Air resistance is included in all three: quadratic drag (Cd), Magnus spin lift capped at a maximum, and spin decay during the flight. Those coefficients are ASSUMED literature values, not POLLEN measurements. The HIVE section is from the official field CAD; tilt 30° is confirmed.

What the model includes. Flight: quadratic drag, Magnus spin lift with a cap and spin decay, RK4 with a 2 ms step. Robot view: the shot flies along the bearing to the cell from the robot's exact spot (whole-field oblique model), and the up cell is 3D (side walls, pentagon roof, lips). The live solver reports two kinds of minimum: knife edge (the lowest clean shot found, a grazing one) and with room for error (still clean if speed and angle drift by the ASSUMED tolerance), each beside the no-spin twin. Options in Advanced settings: venue air (temperature, altitude, humidity; default 20 °C, sea level, dry = 1.204 kg/m³, derived), drag that changes with speed (reference or what-if, ASSUMED), extra drag from backspin (ASSUMED), a steady draft along, across or up the shot (user), a moving robot (speed toward the goal and sideways, turn rate, exit-angle offset d0; user / ASSUMED), a spin-lift table and reverse lift (ASSUMED), air carried with the ball (effective mass, ASSUMED), and a flag on any answer that needs more than the PREDICTED 8.9 m/s grip limit.

Not modelled yet (largest first, from the research ranking; each needs a measurement): the real spin-lift level of POLLEN (Cl); its real drag level (Cd); grip and slip at the wheel (v/u, ball squeeze); the hood angle not being the exit angle; the random side force on a low-spin holed ball (knuckle); exit skew and a tilted spin axis (sideways error); balls and NECTAR already in the cell, bounce (COR) and the HIVE tipping mid-volley; bounces after entry; the air carried inside the ball (effective mass: an ASSUMED option only); flywheel droop and battery voltage at launch. Robot motion and sideways drafts are options (Advanced), not measurements. The "chance it goes in" line is a model probability under ASSUMED launch scatter, not a measured repeatability.

What it is not. No POLLEN drag, spin lift, bounce or launcher grip has been measured. The air cases are literature ranges (pickleball and smooth-sphere data), not POLLEN facts. A shot that touches the HIVE stops here; a bounce can still score (see docs/05). The second HIVE and the A-frame legs are drawn for context only. One clean simulated shot says nothing about repeatability: the tolerance shown is how far speed and angle can drift and still go in. In the Robot view the release point is the robot centre at your height (an offset can be set; the turned 18 × 18 in chassis must stay inside the walls and off the A-frame base) and the turret is assumed to aim exactly (ASSUMED). The Side view and the Field map are precomputed 2D sections at 1.20 kg/m³: their side walls are not modelled, so their windows from a bearing of 35.57° or more are optimistic.
