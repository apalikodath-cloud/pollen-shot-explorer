# Fact-check: ito2025 (Ito & Kamijima 2025, TJSST 17(1) 25-31)

Checked 2026-10-30 against the full-text PDF from J-STAGE
(https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/tjsst/17/1/17_25/_pdf/-char/en, 7 pages). I extracted the text with
`pdftotext -enc UTF-8`, so the Japanese body was readable. Figure 4 was rendered at 400 dpi and digitized by pixel.
I also checked the J-STAGE landing page. Every table cell was compared with the PDF text, and every derived number
was recomputed in Python.

**CHECK: 27 confirmed / 2 corrected / 0 dropped / 10 added** (out of the 29 extracted data points).
There are also 5 corrections to the key equations and the applicability text. They are listed below.

## Verdict on the extraction

The extraction is good. All five tables (SP, C_D, C_L, C_M, C_m) match the PDF exactly in all 180 cells. All 11
figure-only (C_D, C_L) points from Fig. 4 match my pixel digitization to within ±0.005 (the extractor quoted
±0.01-0.02), and the 105-150 rps SP values are correct.

The statements in the text (validation against the Miyazaki-2017 *fitted curves*, C_D about 0.07 higher, C_L about
equal, the dip shifted to larger SP, and the featured Re 26500 / SP 0.565 case) are all in Sec. 3.2 and 3.3.

The inferred Magnus normalization C_M = (3/8)·C_L/SP holds in all 36 cells: the ratio is 0.3730-0.3788, mean 0.3753.

## Corrections

1. **Lift-crisis onset numbers (data point corrected).** The extraction said the dip starts at "about 0.6 at Re 53000
   and about 0.75 at Re 40000". The paper gives no numbers, only that the dip starts at smaller SP as Re rises. The
   tables and Fig. 4 give these values:

   | Re | C_L peak | C_L trough | C_D peak | C_D minimum |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 26500 | 0.318 at SP 0.565 | 0.207 at SP 0.942 | 0.587 at SP 0.754 | still falling at 1.131 (0.510) |
   | 40000 | 0.288 at SP 0.503 (flat to 0.628) | 0.183 at SP 0.880 | 0.568 at SP 0.628 | 0.470 at SP 1.005 |
   | 46500 | 0.280 at SP 0.538 | 0.165 at SP 0.862 | 0.555 at SP 0.646 | 0.460 at SP 1.077 |
   | 53000 | 0.271 at SP 0.471 | 0.153 at SP 0.848 | 0.548 at SP 0.566-0.660 | 0.450 at SP 0.942 |

   So at Re 40000 the decline starts at about SP 0.5-0.63, not 0.75. The steep fall lies between about 0.75 and 0.88.

   The extraction also said there is "only a mild dip at Re 6600-13000". The paper does not say this. The tables show
   C_L dips of 0.06-0.07: 0.373 → 0.310 at SP 1.51 (Re 6600) and 0.338 → 0.267 at SP 1.13 (Re 13000). These dips are
   about as large as the higher-Re ones in relative terms, and they agree with the onset moving to larger SP at lower
   Re. Each is a single grid cell, so it could also be noise. C_D at these Re is roughly flat at 0.58-0.62.
2. **Spin decay scaled to POLLEN (data point corrected).** The recomputed rate is k = 0.0114-0.0229 1/s, which gives
   τ = 44-88 s (the extraction said 43-86 s). The inputs were ρ = 1.2-1.2044, R = 35.5 mm, m = 25 g,
   I = (2/3)mR², v = 5 m/s, S = 1.13 and C_m = 0.0181 (the 45 rps, 5 m/s cell). The difference is minor.

   Note that the Ito cell used is at Re 13000, while POLLEN at 5 m/s is at Re 23600. The paper says C_m depends only
   weakly on Re, so this mismatch is acceptable.
3. **Key equation "torque T ~ ω^1.6-1.7" (corrected).** At fixed V, T ∝ C_m, and SP ∝ ω, so the exponent equals the
   per-speed SP exponent. It ranges from 1.22 to 1.67: 1.67, 1.64, 1.57, 1.42, 1.26 and 1.22 at 2.5, 5, 10, 15, 17.5
   and 20 m/s. The value 1.6-1.7 holds only for V ≤ 5 m/s, and about 1.57-1.67 for V ≤ 10 m/s (the POLLEN band). The
   overall fit C_m = 0.0131·SP^1.73 is confirmed (my fit: 0.01307·SP^1.728).
4. **Key equation "C_m·V² roughly constant in V at fixed ω" (qualified).** The numbers behind it are confirmed. The
   claim only holds at 45-90 rps, where the spread is within about ±12%. At 15 rps C_m·V² varies 2.2x
   (0.069 → 0.152), and at 30 rps it varies 1.6x.
5. **Mesh, y+ and applicability details:**
   - **y+.** "y+ < 1" holds over the whole surface only for Re ≤ 40000. At Re 53000 it holds over at least 99.9% of
     the surface (Sec. 2, pp. 26-27).
   - **Smooth-sphere C_D range.** It is 0.45-0.62, not 0.47-0.62: Fig. 4 has C_D = 0.450 at Re 53000, SP 0.942.
   - **Example acceleration.** C_L 0.3 at 5 m/s gives 0.71 m/s² with ρ = 1.2 (0.73 only with ρ = 1.225).
   - **Spin-axis force.** "Pure backspin produces negligible side force" is a qualitative statement from the
     flow-visualization case, Fig. 6(b). The paper says the force along the spin axis, called 上下方向 (up/down)
     in its figure orientation, "hardly arises regardless of Re". The paper never tabulates F_y.
   - **Dates.** J-STAGE lists "Accepted: May 19, 2025", while the PDF prints it as 再受付 (re-received). Released on
     J-STAGE June 13, 2025 is confirmed.

## What a POLLEN user must know (applicability check)

- **Coverage gap.** At Re ≥ 26500 (POLLEN at about 5.6-10 m/s) the tables stop at SP 1.13, and the figure at SP 1.26.
  SP > 1.1 is covered only at Re ≤ 13000 (2.5-5 m/s for a 40 mm ball). A POLLEN launch at 6-10 m/s with S of 1-2
  therefore lies **outside** the data.
- Coefficient definitions (C_D, C_L, C_M, C_m) are not printed. They are delegated to refs [8, 15]. The C_M form is
  inferred from the data to within 1%. The normalization length for C_m is unresolved.
- Everything else in the original applicability and model sections checks out arithmetically:
  - Re_POLLEN = 4714·v.
  - m/A is 6.31 kg/m² for POLLEN and 2.15 kg/m² for the TT ball, a ratio of 2.94.
  - The force under-prediction from misusing C_M is 2.67x at S = 1.
  - Spin loss is about 3.4% over 1.5 s.
  - The maximum C_L is 0.418.
- No coefficient in this paper is a POLLEN measurement (HANDOFF §5).

## Review status

Peer-reviewed journal (Transactions of the Japan Society for Simulation Technology, 論文誌). J-STAGE does not say so
explicitly, but the article went through a received → revised/accepted cycle. It is a numerical CFD study, and the
authors claim only qualitative agreement (定性的な一致) with experiment.
