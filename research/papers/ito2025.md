## 3. Method (what was actually computed)

| Item | Value (as stated in §2–3.1) |

| Turbulence model | Smagorinsky–Lilly LES. The authors' earlier paper [8] used standard k–ε, which they say did not capture the boundary layer. |
| Setup | Virtual wind tunnel: a **fixed** sphere spinning at a **constant imposed** ω_y about the y-axis, with uniform inflow V_x along +x. Lift is F_z. |
| Domain | Cylinder, L_x = L_x1 + L_x2 = 0.15 + 0.5 = 0.65 m (0.15 m upstream, 0.5 m downstream), radius r_yz = 0.2 m. Blockage ratio set to 1 %. Ball at (0, 0, 0). |

| Boundary-layer mesh | First cell height 0.01 mm, stretch ratio 1.1, 40 layers, surface cell size 0.25 mm. About 10.44 million cells. |
| Time step | 5 µs. SIMPLE pressure–velocity coupling. |
| y⁺ check | y⁺ < 1.0 over the whole surface for Re ≤ 40 000. At Re = 53 000, more than 99.9 % of the surface has y⁺ < 1.0. |
| Air | μ = 1.814×10⁻⁵ N·s/m², ρ = 1.2044 kg/m³ (1 atm, 293.15 K), ν = μ/ρ = 1.506×10⁻⁵ m²/s |
| Test matrix | V_x = 2.5, 5, 10, 15, 17.5, 20 m/s × ω_y = 15, 30, 45, 60, 75, 90 rps (94.2–565.5 rad/s), a full 36-cell grid. Extra cases at 105, 120, 135 and 150 rps for Re = 40 000, 46 500 and 53 000 are shown **in Figs 3–4 only**, not in the tables. |

## 4. Equations

Only Eqs (1) and (2) are printed in the paper. The coefficient definitions are delegated to refs [8] and [15], which I did not read. Items 3–6 below are therefore **my inferences or standard forms** and are labelled as such.

1. **Reynolds number** (Eq. 1, printed):
   `Re = 2 r_b V_x / ν`, with ν = μ/ρ.
   r_b = ball radius [m]; V_x = free-stream speed [m/s]; ν = kinematic viscosity [m²/s].
   Check: r_b = 0.02 m and V_x = 2.5 m/s give Re = 6639.5, which matches the paper.
2. **Spin parameter** (Eq. 2, printed):
   `SP = r_b ω_y / V_x`, with ω_y in **rad/s** (ω = 2π × rps).
   Table 1 reproduces from this formula to within 0.3 %.
3. **Drag and lift coefficients** (standard form; not printed, but consistent with the paper's use):
   `F_D = ½ ρ A V² C_D` and `F_L = ½ ρ A V² C_L`, with A = π r_b² [m²].
4. **Magnus coefficient C_M** (definition inferred, verified numerically). Across all 36 table cells, C_M·SP/C_L = 0.3730–0.3788 (mean 0.3753), so **C_M = (3/8)·C_L/SP**. This is exactly the relation that gives

   `F_M = C_M · ρ · (4/3)π r_b³ · ω V`, i.e. the vector form **F_M = C_M ρ 𝒱 (ω × v)**, where 𝒱 is the ball volume.

   So **C_M carries no new information**: it is C_L divided by SP and rescaled. The authors use C_L for validation and C_M for their trajectory equations of motion (refs [2, 8]).
   ⚠ **Pitfall:** C_M must never be inserted into ½ρAV²·C. PM-001 uses `F_Magnus = ½ρ Cl A |v|² n_M`, so use **C_L** from this paper, not C_M.
5. **Fluid (spin-damping) torque coefficient C_m.** The normalization is **not stated**, and refs [8] and [15] were not read. The data do show that it is normalized by V². At fixed ω, C_m·V² is roughly constant across V: at 90 rps, C_m·V² = 1.39, 1.34, 1.52, 1.48, 1.42 and 1.42 m²/s² for V = 2.5 to 20 m/s. Two candidate forms:
   - (a) `T_m = ½ ρ A V² (2 r_b) C_m`
   - (b) `T_m = ½ ρ A V² r_b C_m` (= ½ρπr_b³V² C_m)

   These differ by a factor of 2 and **cannot be distinguished from this paper**.
6. **Spin decay ODE implied** (my construction, not in the paper):
   `I_b dω/dt = −T_m = −½ ρ π r_b² L V² C_m(SP, Re)`, with L = 2r_b or r_b per (a) or (b).
   - Power-law fit to Table 5 (mine; all 36 cells, log–log least squares): `C_m ≈ 0.0131 · SP^1.73`. The fit is better done per speed:

     | V [m/s] | 2.5 | 5 | 10 | 15 | 17.5 | 20 |
     |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
     | Fitted C_m | 0.0180 SP^1.67 | 0.0142 SP^1.64 | 0.0113 SP^1.57 | 0.0085 SP^1.42 | 0.0070 SP^1.26 | 0.0064 SP^1.22 |

   - Physical reading: **T_m ≈ f(ω), with only weak dependence on V**. T_m scales roughly as ω^1.6–1.7. This means spin damping is set mainly by surface speed, not by flight speed.

### Qualitative findings (§3.2)
- **C_D and C_L both rise, then fall, then rise again as SP increases.** In the first rise they follow **one Re-independent monotone curve**. They then leave that curve, dip, and join a higher-spin rising branch.
- **This is the lift crisis. The higher the Re, the smaller the SP at which the dip starts:**
  - about SP 0.6 at Re 53 000
  - about SP 0.75 at Re 40 000
  - about SP 0.75–0.94 at Re 26 500
  - Re 6600 and 13 000 show at most a mild dip, near SP 0.75–1.5, with C_L 0.27–0.42 throughout.
- **No negative C_L** appears in the tabulated range: the minimum is 0.073.
- **C_m is nearly independent of Re** when plotted against SP, and it increases monotonically with SP.
- **C_M falls with spin and with decreasing speed.** This is purely because C_M ∝ C_L/SP.

## 7. Applicability to POLLEN

**What transfers:**
- **Regime overlap is nearly exact.** POLLEN has Re = 2R·v/ν ≈ 4714·v [s/m], so v = 2–10 m/s gives Re ≈ 9.4×10³–4.7×10⁴. That lies inside Ito's 6.6×10³–5.3×10⁴. POLLEN's S ≈ 0–2 lies inside Ito's 0.094–4.5. This is the only source in the set that maps a **dense (Re, S) grid across exactly our band**, including the low-speed, high-S corner near apex. That corner (v ≈ 2–5 m/s, S ≈ 1–2) is where PM-001's "apex" question lives.
- **Structural lessons transfer as hypotheses:**
  - C_L(S) is non-monotone near Re 2.5–5×10⁴ (lift crisis), so a single monotone parametric Cl(S) can be wrong in shape, not only in scale.
  - C_D rises by about 0.05–0.12 with spin.
  - Side force is negligible for pure backspin.
  - Spin-damping torque is mainly a function of ω.
- **Mass-to-area penalty (geometry plus HANDOFF mass, independent of coefficients).**
  - m/A is 6.31 kg/m² for POLLEN (25 g, R = 35.5 mm) versus 2.15 kg/m² for a 2.7 g TT ball (TT mass assumed, not from the paper).
  - **For equal coefficients, POLLEN's aerodynamic acceleration is about 2.9× smaller than a TT ball's.**
  - Example, if POLLEN had Ito's smooth-sphere C_L: C_L = 0.3 at 5 m/s gives Magnus acceleration ≈ 0.73 m/s² (≈7 % g). C_L = 0.35 at 3 m/s gives ≈ 0.31 m/s².

**What does not transfer:**
- **POLLEN is perforated and hollow; Ito's ball is a smooth, closed sphere.** Holes trip or perturb the boundary layer and allow flow through the shell, as wiffle-ball studies show (see rossmann2007). Those are exactly the mechanisms behind the lift crisis Ito computes. **The crisis dip, the drop-onset S and even the sign of the low-S lift may be entirely different for POLLEN.** Do not use Ito's C_L/C_D values as POLLEN values (HANDOFF §5).
- **CFD is not experiment.** The agreement is qualitative only:
  - C_D is +0.07 versus flight fits, and the crisis is shifted in SP.
  - Averaging over one revolution leaves visible scatter. For example, at 10 m/s C_L goes 0.318 → 0.282 → 0.207 → 0.246 for 45–90 rps; it is unclear whether that is physics or noise.
  - No uncertainty is reported.
  - The ball is fixed in a steady stream with constant spin, so there is no deceleration history. The authors themselves blame part of the mismatch with flight data on this.
- **The torque normalization is unknown** (factor 2), and a perforated shell will almost certainly have a much larger spin-damping torque than a smooth sphere (the holes act as paddles). The smooth-sphere numbers below are a **lower bound** on POLLEN spin decay, not an estimate.

## 8. Implications for the PM-001 models

1. **Aero coefficients (4A).**
   - Add a **"smooth-sphere reference" lookup Cl(S, Re) and Cd(S, Re)** built from Tables 2–3 plus the Fig. 4 points. Use it as one bounding scenario alongside the parametric Cl(S) and Cd = const. Label it `ito2025_smooth_sphere_CFD (uncalibrated for POLLEN)`.
   - Interpolate bilinearly in (log Re, S). Clamp outside S ∈ [0.09, 4.5] and Re ∈ [6.6e3, 5.3e4].
   - This yields a physically plausible **non-monotone** Cl that tests whether the feasibility conclusions survive a lift-crisis dip near S ≈ 0.6–0.9 at v ≈ 6–11 m/s. Those are typical exit speeds, so the dip would act early in the shot, not at the apex.
2. **Cl ceiling.** In this CFD, smooth-sphere C_L never exceeds about 0.42 for S up to 4.5 at Re ≥ 6.6×10³, and it sits at 0.31–0.42 for S ≳ 1.5 at low Re (6600–13 000). **Use Cl_max ≈ 0.40–0.45 as an upper sanity cap** in sweeps. Raise it only if POLLEN measurements justify it.
3. **Cd(S).** The smooth sphere has C_D 0.47–0.62. Spin raises C_D by 0.05–0.12 before the crisis, and low Re (v = 2.5 m/s) gives about 0.58–0.62. **A constant Cd under-predicts drag for spinning balls at low speed.** Add a Cd(S) option, or at least a sweep of Cd ±0.1.
4. **Force-law hygiene.** Implement the Magnus force with **C_L** in ½ρA|v|². Never use Ito's C_M there: it would under-predict force by a factor of (3/8)/S, which is 2.7× too small at S = 1. Add a unit test: C_M·S/C_L = 0.375 on Ito's data.
5. **Spin decay (4A, optional k_spin).**
   - Smooth-sphere scaling of Ito's C_m to POLLEN (R = 35.5 mm, I ≈ (2/3)mR² = 2.1×10⁻⁵ kg·m², v = 5 m/s, S ≈ 1.13 with C_m = 0.0181):
     - normalization (a): k = T/(Iω) ≈ 0.023 s⁻¹, τ ≈ 43 s
     - normalization (b): k ≈ 0.012 s⁻¹, τ ≈ 86 s
   - **Conclusion:** for a smooth sphere, spin decay over a flight of ≤1.5 s is <4 % and negligible. **For perforated POLLEN this is only a lower bound.** Keep k_spin as a sweep, e.g. 0–1 s⁻¹, until two-station high-speed-camera spin measurements exist.
   - If a physics form is wanted, use T = K·ω^n with n ≈ 1.6–1.7 (from Ito's C_m·V² scaling) rather than linear damping, and calibrate K on POLLEN.
6. **Apex / "spin hook" (4B).** Ito shows that the low-v, high-S corner (S > 1.5, Re < 1.5×10⁴) has the highest C_L, about 0.3–0.42. But the force there scales with v², and v is smallest near the apex. With POLLEN's m/A, **even C_L = 0.4 at 3 m/s gives only about 0.35 m/s² (~3.6 % g)**. This supports evaluating the Magnus effect over the whole trajectory rather than expecting an apex "hook".
7. **3D / sidespin.** For pure backspin about one axis, the CFD finds negligible side force. The 2D-first plan is consistent with this. Any POLLEN side force would come from asymmetry in the holes or the shape, not from Magnus.
8. **Measurement priority this source motivates.** Measure POLLEN Cl(S) and Cd(S) directly; drop-tests and fitting two-camera trajectories are enough. Do it at v ≈ 3, 6 and 9 m/s and S ≈ 0.3, 0.7, 1.0 and 1.5. These points bracket the region where the smooth-sphere crisis would appear, and they decide whether POLLEN behaves like Ito's ball or like a wiffle ball.

## 9. Caveats

- The coefficient definitions for C_D, C_L, C_M and C_m are not printed. The C_M form is inferred to about 1 % precision. **The C_m normalization is unresolved (factor 2).**
- Fig. 4 values were digitized by eye at ±0.01–0.02. The figure-only points at 105–150 rps are not in the tables.
- The comparison with "experiment" uses fitted curves from Miyazaki 2017, not raw data. The authors claim only qualitative agreement, and the C_D offset is +0.07.
- One-revolution averaging and the scatter between neighbouring cells suggest an uncertainty of perhaps ±0.03–0.05 in C_L. That is my judgement; the paper gives none.
- The TT ball mass (2.7 g) and the thin-shell inertia used in the derived checks are my assumptions, not values from this paper.
