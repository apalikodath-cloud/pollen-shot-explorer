## 2. What the source actually reports

**Machine.** It is a **two-wheel** pitcher. Two angle grinders each drive one wheel. The photo labels describe them as "35A compliant wheels for grip on ball" and "Angle grinders to apply high RPM, low torque to balls" (Fig. 30). The wheels sit on either side of the ball path, and the ball is fed through a cut PVC tube (Fig. 29). A grinder turns only one way, so one grinder was mounted facing up and the other facing down to get counter-rotation (§5.2). Spin comes from **running the two wheels at different speeds** (§1). Each speed is set on the grinder's own unlabelled dial: "the angle grinders' velocity adjusting panels show no numeric rotating speed" (§6.1). The wooden frame rotates to set the pitch angle. The frame, not the motors, is what pivots (§5.3).

**What "5044 rpm" actually is.** The abstract says the machine has "two spinning wheels whose maximum spinning rate is 5044 rpm". The body shows that this is **not a measurement**. It is the output of Model 2 (Fig. 25): RPM = (V_ball / r_ball)·(60 s/min)/(2π), with V_ball = 60 mph = 26.82 m/s and **r_ball = 2 in = 0.0508 m**, giving "5044 rpm". Fig. 19 states the assumption behind it: "ω_ball = V/r_ball" and "ω_ball = ω_wheel". The model therefore uses the **ball** radius in place of the wheel radius, and it assumes that the ball's spin rate equals the wheel's spin rate. (Our recomputation of their arithmetic gives ≈ 5042 rpm, so the difference is rounding.) The authors say they used a model because the grinder dials have no numeric readout.

**Measured or claimed results (final prototype, §7 and abstract):**
- Pitches "with different spins (backspin, topspin, no spin)". Only qualitative. **No spin rate was measured.**
- "the trajectory of the traveling balls can be recorded by naked eye" (abstract). **No trajectory, curve or deflection was measured.** There are no video tracking, plots or lift numbers.
- Highest ball speed "up to 67 mph". **The measurement method is not stated** anywhere in the report (searches for radar, speed gun, camera, video and fps found nothing).
- Accuracy: "Out of 12 tries, the pitching machine hit the target 12 times from 20 feet away". The abstract describes the target as "20' by 20'". The original goal in §5.1 was 90 % on a "3'x3' square target". The two target sizes are inconsistent, and only the 20 ft × 20 ft one appears with the result.
- Model 1 (Fig. 24) is a **drag-free, lift-free** projectile drop calculation ("negligible drag and lift force", §4.4). Inputs: v = 60 mph, D = 20 ft, h = 2.5 ft, θ = 0, t = 0.34 s → hit height 0.54 m.

**Magnus content.** It is only a textbook diagram (Fig. 1) and one citation (Britannica, "Magnus effect"). There is no lift coefficient, no spin parameter and no spin-versus-curve data.

## 5. Transfer cautions to POLLEN

- **Different architecture.** This is a **two powered wheel** differential pitcher. PM-001 is one powered 98 mm flywheel with a fixed hood or a passive upper roller (HANDOFF §2, §5). Their spin control (a speed difference between two powered wheels) is exactly the dual-powered approach that is out of scope. **It is not a reason to change the baseline.**
- **Different ball.** The ball is a standard Wiffle (radius 2 in as used in their model, slotted, mass not given). POLLEN is a 71 mm, ~25 g, round-hole perforated polyethylene ball. The perforation geometry differs, so Wiffle aerodynamics (Rossmann & Rau 2007, source #3) do not transfer numerically. This report adds no aerodynamic numbers anyway.
- **Different speed regime.** It runs at about 60–67 mph (≈ 27–30 m/s) on a near-horizontal line drive over 20 ft. A HIVE lob is much slower and much more lofted.
- **The kinematic model must not be copied.** "ω_ball = ω_wheel" and "RPM = V/r_ball" are not valid launcher relations. For a single wheel plus a stationary hood, the ideal no-slip relations in source #4 (Brettle) are v_exit ≈ ω_w R_w / 2 and ω_ball ≈ v_exit / R_ball, and even those are idealisations (HANDOFF §5: do not treat v_exit = u_wheel/2 as universal).

**What transfers qualitatively:**
1. A perforated plastic ball launched between compliant (35A) wheels picks up enough spin for a naked-eye visible change in trajectory at high speed. This is weak observational support that perforated balls show Magnus-type behaviour. Rossmann & Rau's reviewed work is the better source for that point.
2. Soft, grippy wheels were chosen on purpose for grip ("without fear of slipping", §6.3). That is consistent with the slip question in our hood and roller contact model (see allen2010.md §7.4). Slip was not measured here.
3. An uncalibrated spin knob is a known failure mode. The team could not say what rpm they ran. **Our test plan must measure wheel speed (encoder) and ball spin (high-speed video) directly.**

## 6. What it means for our model and test plan

- **Model: no inputs.** It provides no C_L, C_D, spin or exit-speed map. Do not cite it for any number. Cite it only as an unreviewed build precedent that a perforated ball curves visibly with wheel-imparted spin.
- **Single versus two wheels:** the report does not compare them. It built only a two-wheel machine and gives no single-wheel data, so it says nothing about whether one 98 mm flywheel plus a hood can reach a useful spin parameter S for POLLEN.
- **Test plan items it reinforces (our design, not theirs):**
  1. Log the flywheel encoder speed before and after each shot. The wheel speed drop matters (Brettle and Pedersen, sources #4 and #5).
  2. Measure POLLEN exit speed and spin from ≥ 240 fps video (mark the ball and count frames per revolution). Report S = ωR/|v|.
  3. Track the trajectory in 2D against a drag-only fit to separate Magnus deflection from drag. "Visible by naked eye" is not evidence of HIVE entry.
  4. Record the wheel material and durometer and the hood material, since grip is a design choice.
