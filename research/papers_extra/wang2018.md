## 2. What the paper reports (abstract, Crossref/Semantic Scholar text)

> "When a table tennis ball is hit by a racket, the ball spins and undergoes a complex trajectory in the air. In this article, a model of a spinning ball is proposed for simulating and predicting the ball flight trajectory including the topspin, backspin, rightward spin, leftward spin, and combined spin. The actual trajectory and rotational motion of a flying ball are captured by three high-speed cameras and then reconstructed using a modified vision tracking algorithm. For the purpose of model validation, the simulated trajectory is compared to the reconstructed trajectory, resulting in a deviation of only [single percentage figure withheld: abstract-only rule]. Such high modeling accuracy makes this proposed method an ideal tool for developing the virtual vision systems emulating the games that can be used to train table tennis players efficiently."

Findings, graded:
1. **Peer-reviewed finding (qualitative, abstract only).** A rig with three synchronised high-speed cameras and a tracking algorithm can reconstruct **both** the 3D trajectory **and** the rotational motion of a small spinning ball in free flight.
2. **Peer-reviewed finding (qualitative, abstract only).** A spinning-ball flight model can reproduce reconstructed trajectories for topspin, backspin, side spin and combined spin, closely enough that the authors call it high accuracy. **The deviation metric is not defined in the abstract.** It could be a percentage of path length, of landing distance or of something else. So the headline figure cannot be compared with anything, and we do not carry it.
3. **Not visible:** the frame rate, the camera layout, how spin was measured, any Cd/Cl/spin-decay values, the spin range, the speed range and the number of shots.

## 3. Method (as far as the abstract allows)

- Three high-speed cameras, so multi-view 3D reconstruction is possible. Three views also allow a spin axis with any orientation, including side spin and combined spin.
- A "modified vision tracking algorithm" extracts both the ball centre (translation) and the rotation. The rotation is probably taken from surface features, but the abstract does not say whether these are the printed logo, added markers or something else. **Unverified.**
- Validation by comparing trajectories: the simulated path from the measured initial state against the reconstructed path.

## 6. Applicability to POLLEN

**Transfers (qualitatively):**
- **Spin and trajectory can both come from video alone.** No radar or spin sensor is needed. That supports the PM-001 plan to fit C_D and C_L(S) for POLLEN from side + overhead video.
- **More than one view is needed for a general spin axis.** Wang et al. used three cameras so that side spin and combined spin are observable. With our two views (side + overhead), backspin about a near-horizontal axis perpendicular to the flight plane is observable from the side camera. Side spin, or a tilted axis caused by a misaligned flywheel or hood, shows up only as lateral drift in the overhead view, with no direct spin reading. **Treat any lateral curvature in the overhead view as a sign of a tilted spin axis.** Do not assume pure backspin.
- **Validate by comparing whole trajectories.** Their validation is: simulate from the measured initial state, then compare with the reconstructed path. That is the right acceptance test for our model. We should state the error in **millimetres at the CELL plane**, not as an undefined percentage.

**Does not transfer:**
- Any coefficient. A table-tennis ball is a small, light, smooth, sealed celluloid or plastic shell, and in play it spins much faster relative to its speed than POLLEN will. POLLEN is a 71 mm, ~25 g, perforated polyethylene shell. Its holes change the boundary layer and may give a different, possibly non-monotonic or reversed, C_L(S) (compare sources #3 Rossmann & Rau and #11 Cross & Lindsey 2017). **No table-tennis value may be used as a POLLEN value.**
- The camera settings. Table-tennis spin and speeds need much higher frame rates than POLLEN at 5–8 m/s probably does. Size the rig for POLLEN (cross_lindsey2013.md §7).

## 7. Implications for the PM-001 model and test plan

1. **Rig geometry:** side camera with its line of sight along the intended spin axis, plus an overhead camera. This is a minimum. If budget allows, add a third, oblique camera (Wang et al. used three) to recover the spin-axis tilt directly. Without it, infer axis tilt from overhead-view lateral curvature through the fitted model (a side-force coefficient C_Y as a free parameter).
2. **Model validation metric:** for each test shot, fit or measure the initial state (position, velocity, spin vector) from the first ~0.1 s of flight. Integrate the PM-001 flight model forward, then report the miss distance at the HIVE CELL plane in mm, plus the arrival angle. Accept the model only if the spread of miss distances is small compared with the CELL opening. Report the median and the worst case over ≥ 20 shots. Never judge from one shot (HANDOFF §5).
3. **Spin extraction:** follow cross_lindsey2013.md §7 (marker pattern, frames per revolution). This paper gives no method detail we can copy.
4. **Do not** cite Wang et al. for any number. Cite it only for "three-camera high-speed tracking of trajectory and spin of a small ball is feasible, and a drag + Magnus model can be validated against it".
